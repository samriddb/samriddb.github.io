I spent my middle and high school years in Doha, Qatar, where I went to an Indian school. Like almost all other Indian immigrant families, mine moved here in search for a better life. My parents decided to put me in an Indian school cause they believed the curriculum to be more rigorous and they wanted me to be in tune with my culture. So, I did the notorious CBSE curriculum.

College applications is unfortunately a flawed system that has measurable impact on your career and life. Everyone grows emotionally, academically, professionally and socially during college. 

# Some disclaimers

Some disclaimers: I may not know what I'm talking about. As I'm writing this, I'm 20 years old and I know that the college application process and high school experience has changed significantly since when I was doing this (especially with the pandemic and AI). I still haven't finished university. But, here's what I do know - I did college applications for multiple countries completely by myself.

I also really dislike when people brag about their college admissions so I will choose to not make a list of colleges that I got into or scholarships that I received. I don't think my admissions say anything about what specific colleges look for as this number is just not statistically significant. 

## If you are in high school
This is by no means a list of things you should or shouldn't do. We all have different backgrounds, upbrinings, goals and priorities. I just hope you can draw some insight from what I was thinking going through this process and what I would do differently. 

## If you are not in high school
Enjoy this read! I invite you to use this as an opportunity to reflect on your own school and college experience. I can only hope that the working of my 17 year old brain is mildly interesting to you.

# Why I decided to study abroad
Some of the many reasons:
-  I wasn't inclined to go through the rat race that is the Indian university standardized exams, considered some of the most notorious in the world. For those unfamiliar, about xyz students write the Joint Entrance Examiniations (JEE) every year, where they're tested on Physics, Mathematics and Chemistry. The top smth students get admissions into the IITs, the most presitigous schools in India.
- I wasn't acclimiated to life in India.
- I love Qatar but I didn't want to spend my university life there, or anywhere in the Gulf for that matter.
- I want to meet people from all over the world.
- I wanted to go to bigger universities. Compare the course catalog of CMU vs CMU Qatar.
- I wanted the option to take whatever class I could dream of. At CMU, I took a class on Chinese Cultural practices, where we learned about different types of hotpot and performed chinese dances. Another fun one was a class on esoteric programming languages, where we developed a mock esolang of our own. These experiences were important for me. 


# What do you want out of university

You need to think about what kind of people you want to surround yourself with for the next four years. Plenty of people come in with ambitions of doing grad school, leaving to form a startup, etc. but find that the environment at their university just doesn't suit them - causing them to feel demotivated. 

If you're confident in your will and motivation, then ignore my advice above. If you have a will that strong, then you'll be able to live through not enjoying the people you'll spend your time with, the weather of your city, the campus architecture, or other details that might seem trite. I must point out however that keep in mind, you will change. You don't have to go to classes anymore. Your parents will know less about your life now. There are less extrinsic motivations now.

## Extra-curricular activities

It is here I will provide the ground-breaking advice that has been iterated to every single high schooler -- do what you enjoy.

Throughout my schooling, I've picked up and dropped more hobbies than I can remember. I asked my parents to put me into Karate lessons because I wanted to be stronger than my elder sister. I've always loved music so I learned guitar and various percussion instruments over the years and played in a band in high school. I had various periods of not playing the guitar for months a time and thinking it wasn't for me and then picking it back up again. My family and I would watch the reality show 'Dance India Dance' together when I was still in kindergarden, which inspired me to learn dance, only to quit it 5 years later. Growing up playing chess with my family, I also played for my school. I always though tennis looked cool, 

The word hobby is overloaded. You don't need to take art lessons to claim that art is your hobby. If you doodle in classes and that is the exact level of committment that you want from art, then just do that. Don't try to label yourself too much -- this will only lead you to miss out on opportunities in life. I even remember picking up drums in the seventh grade thinking it was too late for me because so many people at school had been playing since they were much younger. This is a theme that will stick through the rest of your life and you have to learn to get rid of that feeling. Playing tabla for my school was one of the most enriching experiences of my life.
